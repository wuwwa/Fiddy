# Refinement review

This review records measured interaction issues and the order of the next improvements. The collection stays local, keeps its minimal interface, and preserves hidden material fatigue rather than counting presses.

## Changes already verified

- **Jelly cleanup after visual feedback.** Removed the air-pocket system, its sounds and pressure query, the additional depth pass, procedural bump texture, and scattering extension. Jelly again uses the shared physical material with a lighter strawberry tint and lower-intensity studio lighting. The rounded body has a softer outline. Surface contact compliance was tightened slightly so the softer shape keeps the established upward-pull response; the original visible-travel assertion remains intact. Pressing, stretching, multi-touch, fatigue, and continuous rupture recovery remain. Final browser captures are `surface-{webgpu,webgl}-after-jelly-*.png`; graphics counts are back to 16 geometries and 10 textures through recovery and resize.

- **Putty retains a bounded kneaded shape.** A separate, optional plastic reference cage learns only after sustained local strain, with volume projection before acceptance. The current skin and velocity never jump when the reference changes. A four-second front hold left about 0.129 units of visible indentation after recovery; a diagonal pull retained about 0.245 units of contact travel. Thirty changing single-grip pulls left the measured surface volume within 0.52% of its original volume. Further idle simulation preserved the learned shape, and Reset reproduced the original vertices exactly. Seven physical tests include sixteen five-finger extreme kneading cycles and reduced-motion/fixed-step checks. Quiet dry foley keeps the shared voice and cleanup limits. The new profile is opt-in; Cushion keeps its slow elastic recovery.
- **Gentle two-finger turns release the automatic poke.** Contact-pair rotation eases inward pressure while the existing cage follows both fingertips. The mapping ignores small jitter, distinguishes radial pinches and common translation, and gradually restores pressure after one finger lifts. It adds no global rotation force. A real-skin probe with a 390×844 camera measured a 15° gesture improving from 4.31° to 7.64° on Jelly, 6.69° to 9.91° on Cushion, and 5.28° to 8.29° on Loop. Contact tracking error fell by roughly 70–86%; matched pinches and common translations were exactly unchanged. Partial release did not teleport the skin. `node --import tsx qa/pair-turn-study.ts` reproduces these comparisons, including stronger turns and volume checks.
- **Faster surface updates at the same detail.** Indexed Float32 normal accumulation and combined box/sphere scans replace the generic mesh update in both soft-body animation and rupture recovery. Exact-reference checks cover all five shapes, interpolated deformation, overlapping ripples, degenerate faces, ray intersections, and unchanged buffer/bounds identities. A paired local full-skin benchmark measured 2.787 to 1.634 ms without waves and 6.044 to 3.739 ms with four waves (about 38–41% less CPU time). These are CPU update times, not GPU timings or phone FPS estimates. `node --import tsx qa/surface-bench.ts` reproduces the comparison.
- **Settled triangle seams remain pressable.** Browser validation exposed a real precision miss after Reset: the settled Star's nearly symmetric center vertices can make Three's exact ray test miss both adjacent triangles. A failed pick now retries at a numerical offset far below one CSS pixel and still requires an actual surface intersection. The exact ray is restored afterward. Ordinary hits are unchanged, and tests verify that the Loop hole and outside space stay empty.
- **Quick flicks retain their final movement.** Each grip tracks the raw movement consumed by the fixed solver. Normal release transfers the remaining legal offset into a bounded local velocity impulse, using the same pressure, twist, floor, and upward stretch limits as a held pull. The browser samples changed release coordinates, including releases without a preceding move event. Already-consumed movement and pressure changes alone add no second kick. Cancellation discards pending input; other fingers retain theirs. Reduced motion attenuates the impulse, and the skin stays continuous at release.
- **Faster edge constraints.** The innermost edge norm now uses a direct square root on bounded cage coordinates. In an isolated five-pull Node probe, the step median fell from 0.712 to 0.484 ms; matched trajectories differed by at most 2.5e-14 over 2,000 steps. These CPU measurements do not establish phone frame rate.
- **Independent release with several fingers.** Compression and torsion now use the remaining pressure or turning load when choosing their return spring and damping. Previously, any remaining finger suppressed Jelly's bounce and made Cushion recover too quickly, even if that finger was pressing a different area. New tests compare a partial release against the same material released alone and verify that the remaining grip still works.
- **Less allocation during heavy interaction.** The volume-constraint loop reads its four vertex indices directly. This removes up to 737,280 temporary arrays per simulated second with five active pulls. A local Node benchmark improved from a median 0.762 to 0.629 ms per physics step; this is a CPU benchmark, not a phone frame-rate claim.
- **Orientation changes release grips cleanly.** The soft-body scene cancels pointer and keyboard grips before changing its camera for a new CSS viewport size. It preserves deformation and fatigue, so the material recovers naturally. Internal rendering-resolution changes keep grips intact.
- **Bookmarks match the toy being shown.** Unknown or duplicate toy IDs are corrected in the current history entry. Renderer options, other query values, and URL fragments survive selection and browser navigation.
- **Controls fit short screens.** The shared stage follows the available viewport rather than extending below it. Short layouts retain the functional controls in a compact dock.
- **Collection dismissal stays within reach.** The collection heading and Close button remain available while scrolling through the cards.

## Next priorities

1. **Pause additions and evaluate the existing feel on physical phones.** The user rejected the air-pocket and material experiments; they have been removed. Evaluate the existing press, pull, and release responses before proposing further mechanics.
2. **Profile the remaining skin deformation, ripple, and plastic-flow work.** Normals and bounds now use the faster path. Re-measure before choosing another optimization; preserve both normal passes during ripples so waves follow the deformed skin and highlights remain correct. Small temporary point objects are a lower priority: V8 eliminated their measured allocation in the warmed deform path.
3. **Refine Putty's local shape cues on physical phones.** The current cage creates broad, smooth dents and raised regions. Examine whether smaller fingertip impressions and stronger highlight changes make retained deformation easier to read without weakening its volume bounds or making its dry finish glossy.

Vertical front flicks remain subtler than lateral flicks because the local cage has a fixed base and preserves volume. The retention checks compare against the same movement consumed by one physics step, rather than introducing a whole-body lift that would change the touched material's behavior. A future feel pass should examine that local vertical response on physical phone hardware.

Optional browser haptics remain a later enhancement. First validate subtle contact and rupture pulses on a physical supported Android device. Safari on iPhone has limited native-switch taps, but general custom squish and rupture patterns would use a native integration. Haptics should not delay the current web interaction work.

## Validation and practical limits


`qa/putty-smoke.mjs` checks short and sustained native touches, retained deformation, sleeping with a learned shape, keyboard flattening, two-finger pulling and partial release, mute/stationary audio, orientation interruption, reduced motion, desktop mouse input, exact reset, and constant graphics resources. Run it with `--webgl` for the fallback backend. `qa/putty-study.ts` records actual contact travel and closed-surface volume through holds, long idle periods, and repeated kneading. The browser pass caught and corrected a test-harness race: reset assertions now wait for plastic memory to clear, not merely for a frame with no active contacts.

Pair-turn tests cover either direction, jitter, staggered touch events, radial motion, replacement fingers, coincident/crossing contacts, five-contact limits, invalid input, and fixed-step consistency. `qa/turn-smoke.mjs` validates native trusted two-finger input on Jelly, Cushion, and Loop with actual WebGPU and forced WebGL2: gentle turns, unchanged pinch/translation pressure, partial release, a fresh single press, reset during a turn, orientation cancellation, and stable graphics resources. Its `--interruptions-only` mode checks cancellation, canvas blur, and reduced-motion interaction on Cushion. These browser observations complement the real-skin comparison rather than treating a pressure diagnostic as proof of visible rotation.

The unit suite covers actual skinned deformation, independent multi-touch release, bounded extreme pulls, fatigue, recovery, and lifecycle behavior. Flick checks cover all three axes, unchanged positions at release, consumed-input suppression, pressure-only changes, reversal, event subdivision, recovery at different render rates, reduced motion, and 125 rapid releases per material. `qa/surface-smoke.mjs` verifies all five soft shapes, updated normal buffers, press/stretch/release, two-finger gestures, compressed/twisted Cushion, repeated Jelly rupture, Loop rupture, reset, and constant graphics resources on WebGPU and forced WebGL. `qa/flick-smoke.mjs` verifies trusted mouse/touch endpoint-only releases, cancellation, stationary holds, partial multi-touch, and extreme endpoints on both backends. `qa/resize-smoke.mjs` exercises trusted mouse, touch, and keyboard interruptions during orientation changes, including retained deformation/fatigue, late events, fresh input, reset, and stable CSS sizes during resolution changes. The shared-player browser checks cover route correction, navigation, sound state, short layouts, and collection scrolling.

Browser touch emulation is useful for event and layout checks. It does not establish physical-phone performance, vibration quality, thermal behavior, or battery use; those need a real-device pass.
