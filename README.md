# Fiddy

A local Vite, React, and TypeScript collection with six interactive Three.js soft toys, ASCII Tide, Liquid Light, two starlight studies, Magnetic Dust, Silk, and two cuttable jelly toys. Soft-toy rendering prefers WebGPU and falls back to WebGL2; ASCII Tide and Magnetic Dust use Canvas 2D, Liquid Light uses WebGL2 with floating-point render targets, and Silk, the starlight studies, and cuttable jelly use Three.js WebGL2. Fonts and assets are local; there is no backend, account system, analytics, or deployment integration.

## Run

Requires Node.js 20.19+ or 22.12+ and npm.

```sh
npm install
npm run dev -- --port 5174
```

Open `http://127.0.0.1:5174/`. `npm run dev` uses port 5173 by default.

```sh
npm test
npm run build
npm run preview
```

The static production files are in `dist/`, ready for your deployment.

## Cuttable jelly

- **Jelly Slice — `?toy=jelly-slice`:** Hold to pull a fine cutting cord slowly through rose gel. The default cut runs vertically on screen. The wire's center resists while its ends pull down; the gel indents, then separates when the wire reaches the base. A normal cut takes roughly three seconds; dragging down adds tension and dragging up eases it. Even maximum pressure requires a sustained cut.
- **Jelly Prism — `?toy=jelly-prism`:** A taller mint prism with the same wire interaction and a slightly deeper plop. Both gels have a gently domed surface, smooth bevels, studio reflections, and small refracted air cavities. Each fragment retains its original surface curvature and interior detail after a cut. The cord uses one fixed tube mesh; a faint shared-geometry overlay keeps its submerged section readable.
- **Sound:** A quiet, damp pull builds toward one short, low plop at separation. A small contact sound marks entry. The plop uses a rapidly falling bubble mode that settles around 76 Hz, with a rounded attack and damped secondary lobe. Depth and tension control the quiet friction; completed holds never repeat the plop. The graph contains one reused pull source and at most two transient voices. Muting, cancelling, pausing, and switching fade the sound; the page's sound preference carries between toys. Original synthesized buffers require no external recordings or network requests.
- **Input:** Mouse and one primary touch are supported. Move to position the wire and use the visible angle buttons to rotate it. For keyboard input, use arrows to aim, Q/E to angle, and **hold** Space to pull the wire down. Release before completion to lift it without splitting the block. A completed cut stays cut. R or Reset restores the original solid.
- **Lifecycle:** Reduced motion removes rebound while retaining the user-controlled, slow wire descent. Resizing preserves cuts and releases input. Opening the collection or hiding the page pauses animation and sound. Resting scenes stop scheduling frames. Each session disposes its graphics and audio, with a short fade before the audio context closes.
- **Performance:** `src/slicing/gel.ts` batches every fragment into one fixed geometry and one shared physical material. Surface positions and analytic normals upload only after a cut or reset; wobble and pressure deformation run in the vertex shader. A shared half-float back-surface pass measures view depth for absorption, with a fragment-bounds fallback where another fragment occludes that surface. This is a screen-space optical approximation, not full ray tracing. Air cavities and shadows are separate fixed instanced meshes. Cut footprints conserve area; rounded edges and the crown are visual surface shaping. Thin scraps are rejected and the scene remains capped at 48 pieces.
- **Validation:** `npm test` covers area conservation, vertical projection, cut timing across frame rates, pressure resistance, cancelled cuts, repeated cuts, bounds, wire geometry, and fixed graphics buffers. `node qa/slice-smoke.mjs`, with the development server on port 5174, checks WebGL rendering, slow mouse/touch/keyboard cuts, cancellation, reset/pause/resize, reduced motion, sound, and disposal. It compares one and 48 pieces, stress-tests 100 audio cut/reset cycles, and renders audio offline to verify a quiet pull, a distinct low-frequency plop, headroom, and short silent tails. Screenshots and measurements go to `qa/artifacts/`, along with `wire-plop-preview.wav` for listening. Signal checks do not judge subjective sound quality. Touch checks use browser emulation; physical phone hardware has not been tested.

## Starlight studies

Two independently rendered recreations inspired by the interactive artwork on [OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra launch page](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-astra/):

- **Swirl — `?toy=astra-swirl`:** A curled, six-like spiral with fine blue and amber dust, white stars, soft halos, and particles drifting inward along the spiral.
- **Shapes — `?toy=astra-cursor`:** The original cursor plus circle, heart, star, infinity, flower, triangle, square, and wave outlines. Choose a preset or select **Draw a shape**, sketch with a mouse/pen/finger, then **Animate drawing**. Supports up to 16 separate strokes, open paths, self intersections, optional connected ends, undo, and clear. The most recent drawing, selected shape, and speed are saved locally in the browser. Choose **Your drawing** to return to it after trying a preset.
- **Flow speed:** The revised default moves at about 40% of the previous cursor pace (roughly 78 seconds per cursor circuit). A 0.25×–3× slider adjusts travel without jumping the stars or altering their twinkle. Every shape uses the same distance per second; open strokes fade at their endpoints, with no visible bridge back to their start.
- **Pointer/touch:** Drag with the primary pointer or one finger to rotate the cloud using a virtual trackball. Release a moving gesture for damped momentum; holding still before release stops it. Scroll, or use the visible +/− buttons, to zoom. Both studies have direct links in the collection and a Swirl/Shapes switch.
- **Keyboard:** Focus the scene, then use arrows to rotate, Q/E to roll, +/− to zoom, Space to pause/play the ambient motion, and R or Home to reset orientation and zoom. Brief taps and held keys both work.
- **Motion:** Pause freezes the particles and their shimmer. Direct manipulation remains available. Reduced motion starts the artwork still and disables release momentum; explicit Play can opt into particle animation. Opening the collection or hiding the page suspends the renderer. Resizing preserves orientation and zoom while cancelling any active grip.
- **Implementation:** `src/astra/` contains seeded geometry, point-sprite shaders, quaternion rotation with analytical damping, and shared scene/input ownership. Each outline is resampled by arc length into a float texture, with one row per stroke. The vertex shader transports star offsets along each path. Rendering uses two GPU geometries, one shader program, and one path texture for Shapes; switching outlines disposes the previous geometry and texture. Reset does not rebuild them. No reference artwork or remote runtime assets are embedded.
- **Verification:** `npm test` includes geometry finiteness, cursor continuity, deterministic sampling, extreme off-surface drags, quaternion stability, and frame-rate-independent momentum. Open `/qa/astra-smoke.html` and select **Run checks** for actual WebGL rendering, rendered particle travel (measured from GPU pixels), keyboard taps, zoom/reset, pause, reduced motion, resize, abort, and disposal checks. Browser QA also covered mouse drags, collection navigation, and a 390×844 viewport; physical touch hardware has not been tested.

## Field experiences

- **ASCII Tide — `?toy=ascii-tide`:** Move over the character landscape to push it aside. Hold or drag with a finger to gather a vortex, then release to send a wake through neighboring characters. A connected membrane carries the disturbance, with lighting following its height and slope. Stable character identities and six cached glyph tones keep the motion legible. Resizing preserves the wake; Reset restores the original wave field. Reduced motion freezes the ambient waves and softens disturbances.
- **Liquid Light — `?toy=liquid-light`:** Drag to paint luminous fluid; quick taps create blooms. Slow strokes leave ribbons, while fast strokes inject stronger momentum. Colors cycle through cyan, violet, coral, and mint with both time and stroke distance. Midpoint backtracing and limited MacCormack transport preserve fine color boundaries; velocity smoothing and pressure projection keep the released flow smooth. Simulation and dye use separate resolutions with reused graphics buffers. Reset restores a compact full-palette swirl. Reduced motion lowers curl and momentum and shortens persistence.
- **Keyboard:** Focus the field, use arrows to move the visible interaction point, and hold Space to gather characters or paint fluid. These two experiences are silent and use one primary pointer or finger. Their controls do not change the soft toys' multi-touch behavior.
- Both fields pause while the collection is open or the document is hidden. Their independent, lazy-loaded modules share input and lifecycle utilities in `src/fields/`; switching releases listeners, animation frames, and owned graphics resources. Liquid Light reports missing WebGL2 or float-texture support through the player's error/retry interface.

## Magnetic Dust and Silk

- **Magnetic Dust — `?toy=magnetic-dust`:** Press into an uneven bed of 9,600 graphite-colored iron filings on warm paper. Small cast shadows and directional facets give the grains texture without animated glow. A softened pair of opposite virtual poles approximates a finite dipole: grains align with its field, gather across flux contours with slight individual variation, and feel bounded attraction from the gradient of field strength squared. Dragging moves and turns the magnet; releasing relaxes the chains and leaves a fading impression. Hover and arrow-only aiming remain inactive. Repel is a stylized outward scattering mode using the same material. Reset restores the loose bed and Attract mode; reduced motion softens forces and shortens the impression. This is a field-guided visual model rather than a full grain-to-grain magnetic simulation. The paper texture is cached on resize and its canvas backing store is released on disposal.
- **Silk — `?toy=silk`:** Catch a patch of fabric and pull that same patch upward and sideways. Warp, weft, and diagonal extension constraints gather surrounding material into folds instead of allowing rubber-like stretch; corrected velocities let it settle through damped ripples after release. Moving brushes create light waves; stationary hover leaves it alone. Fast drags retain the original touched patch, and resizing releases the grip before the camera moves. Pearl, Sand, and Ink finishes use a fine weave, directional sheen, a hem following the deformed surface, and soft shadows without iridescence or rainbow coloring. Color choices persist locally. Reduced motion freezes ambient folds at their current phase and softens interaction. Reset restores the fabric while keeping the selected color.
- Both pages are silent, accept a primary pointer or touch, and share the existing arrow-key interaction marker and Space hold. Collection and page visibility pause rendering; cancellation, reset, and disposal release input. Silk requires WebGL2 and uses the existing retry interface if initialization fails.
- Open `/qa/dust-silk.html` on the development server and select **Run checks** for actual rendering, complete touch strokes, attached grips, color selection, pause/reset, resize, graphics ownership, cancellation, and repeated switching. **Show mobile previews** embeds both pages at 390×844. `/qa/silk-poses.html` holds center and corner pulls for visual inspection and releases them on demand. Unit tests cover extreme forces, lateral gathering and recovery, re-grabs, brush ripples, reduced motion, and camera-to-fabric coordinate mapping. Physical touchscreen hardware has not been tested.

## Soft toys

- **Jelly:** Press the front or sides to dent that face inward; hold longer to sink deeper. Press the top to flatten the body and spread its belly. Pull up or sideways to stretch the touched patch and twist around the grounded base. Moving into a pull smoothly releases automatic pressing, while deliberate inward pushes still compress. Pull resistance builds toward a bounded limit, and catching a rebound preserves its momentum. Release for a springy rebound and fading ripples.
- **Cushion:** A rounded lavender block with denser resistance. Both inward dents and top compression deepen during a sustained hold. Upward and sideways pulls have a generous range while the base stays grounded; resistance recovers gradually on release so the stretched shape eases back instead of snapping.
- **Putty (`?toy=putty`):** A dense material that retains long presses and pulls. Brief taps spring back; sustained strain leaves a bounded dent or raised region. A top squeeze makes it flatter and wider. Reset restores the mould, and leaving the toy ends that session's shape memory. Optional sound uses quiet dry contact accents and rubbing.
- **Loop:** A glossy mint ring with a real opening. Grab its rim, pull it into an oval, or press it inward for an elastic rebound. Pointers through the empty center do not grab the ring.
- **Star:** A puffed butter-yellow star with five pliable points. Stretch a tip or sink into its middle; its softer body returns gently.
- **Dumpling:** A cream bun with twelve modeled shoulder pleats and a gathered top. Holds gradually sink into the dense, doughy body, which slowly returns after release.
- **Material fatigue:** Jelly and Loop can give way after strong deformation is sustained long enough. Hidden fatigue follows actual compression, stretch, and twist; it recovers as the shape relaxes. Brief taps are safe and there is no press counter. Jelly gives way sooner than Loop; Star, Cushion, and Dumpling remain solid. A rupture releases the held skin into a spreading gel puddle, then the same surface recovers over about two seconds. Reduced motion smoothly relaxes the held shape.
- **Touch:** Use two fingers to pinch inward, pull opposite sides apart, or press one area while stretching another. Up to five fingers can hold separate points, each with its own dent and drag direction. Lifting or cancelling one finger leaves the others holding; the canvas keeps pinch gestures on the toy without scrolling or zooming the page. Touchscreens show a short two-finger hint.
- **Keyboard:** Tab to the toy, hold Space to compress, and use the arrow keys while held to stretch in screen directions. Up pulls upward; sideways arrows bend and stretch. Arrows unload pressure just like pointer dragging. While holding Space, Q and E twist in opposite directions. Release Space to let the body recover.
- **Sound:** Initially muted. Enable it for varied soft taps, textured squishes, and a gentle stretch tone that follows hand speed and tension. Releases change with hold time, compression, and stretch; denser materials sound lower and linger longer. Stationary holds fall quiet, and multiple fingers share one moving texture. The sound preference carries across toy switches within the page.
- **Reset:** Restores the undeformed shape and clears stored pressure, twist, momentum, and ripples.
- **Collection:** Switch between all fourteen experiences in a responsive tile picker. Opening it pauses the current toy. Selecting the current toy resumes it without a reset.

Each soft toy grows into view with a brief spring entrance, a soft squash on landing, and a contact shadow, then stays still until touched. Reduced motion skips the entrance and reduces Jelly's rebound. The play surface has functional controls and collection navigation.

Direct links use `?toy=jelly`, `?toy=cushion`, `?toy=loop`, `?toy=star`, or `?toy=dumpling`. Browser Back/Forward works, and unknown IDs open Jelly. Add `&renderer=webgl` to force WebGL2 for testing. WebGPU requires a secure context; localhost qualifies.

## Implementation

- `src/toys/`: lightweight typed definitions, names, icons, themes, instructions, and lazy loaders.
- `src/player/`: shared UI and session ownership, including cancellation, disposal, errors, sound preferences, and pause behavior.
- `src/style.css`: shared floating surfaces, with theme-derived translucent surfaces and fine borders, without drop shadows. Sound and Reset share a capsule beside the Collection button; the collection opens as an inset panel with a scrollable grid and fixed close control. Mobile layouts respect safe areas, and reduced motion disables the panel entrance. Jelly instructions remain visible on desktop and available to screen readers on mobile.
- `src/{jelly,cushion,putty,loop,star,dumpling}/entry.ts`: small adapters into the shared soft-body engine.
- `src/soft-body/profiles.ts`: independently tunable feel, material, shape, inward pressure, compression, press/pull ranges, surface-grab compliance, pull recovery time, torsion, ripple, and audio settings.
- `src/soft-body/physics.ts`: a 125-node volumetric lattice, XPBD edge/volume constraints, surface-normal indentation, spring-driven torsion, and up to five independent compliant surface contacts. Every contact keeps its own hold age, pressure, normal, drag target, and skin binding. Overlapping forces blend without multiplying stiffness; top compression remains bounded, and opposing torques cancel. During pulling, edges can stretch more freely while volume constraints tighten; resistance recovers gradually after release. Additional shared tension limits incompatible pulls from three or more fingers, and below-floor contact targets stay on the floor. The lowest cage row stays fixed and cubic B-spline weights blend smoothly into it near the contact patch. Nonnegative skinning weights avoid sharp ridges. Cage offsets are interpolated once per frame for efficient deformation.
- `src/soft-body/pressure.ts`: invertible, volume-preserving compression and twist maps. The belly spreads slightly more than the crown and base; rotation smoothly fades to zero at the base. Grabs compensate for both the expanded and rotated surface.
- `src/soft-body/plasticity.ts`: optional, bounded material memory. Local strain must persist before reference positions and edge lengths evolve. Volume projection redistributes material; invalid reference cells back off without changing the visible skin. Retained top compression is bounded separately. Idle learned shapes can sleep, and Reset clears both geometry memory and dwell.
- `src/soft-body/geometry.ts`: smooth, welded surfaces with roughly 8,000–10,000 vertices per toy. Pebble, cushion, five-arm star, and pleated dumpling surfaces are sculpted from an evenly tessellated sphere; Loop uses a torus with a true hole. All shapes have rounded contact patches. Keyboard pressing starts on each shape's actual upper surface.
- `src/soft-body/surface.ts`: deforms the skin, applies ripples along its current normals, and updates the final lighting normals and bounds.
- `src/soft-body/input.ts`: transforms world pointer movement into local drag distance without adding motion from the entrance animation, and smoothly separates stationary/inward pressing from outward or tangential pulling.
- `src/soft-body/pointers.ts`: bounded, independent pointer capture records. Each finger has its own anchor and drag plane; release and cancellation affect only that pointer. Reset, blur, hidden pages, pause, and disposal clear all captures. Keyboard uses a separate reserved contact and cannot replace active fingers.
- `src/soft-body/activity.ts`: waits for sustained rest before stopping animation frames, with immediate wake for input, reset, resize, and resume.
- `src/soft-body/scene.ts`: WebGPURenderer, input, fixed camera, updated normals, pause/resume, and adaptive resolution. Jelly uses a physical node material with a refracted chord estimate for absorption depth. Color remains present at grazing angles instead of fading into a pale rim. There is no distance fog, so the finish stays consistent when the camera moves farther away on narrow screens.
- `src/soft-body/entrance.ts`: bounded, one-time appearance animation with reduced-motion support.
- `src/soft-body/reactions.ts`: hidden fatigue integrates measured strain over time, with recovery under relaxed deformation and independent material thresholds. Input event counts have no effect. Rupture releases every grip; fresh touches work again after recovery.
- `src/soft-body/burst-visual.ts`: the existing skin releases from the most strained patch into a softly lobed puddle, settles, and smoothly recovers. The body stays visible with its original material. Reusable surface buffers avoid new meshes, materials, particles, or graphics allocations; reduced motion uses a gradual relaxation of the held deformation.
- `src/soft-body/lighting.ts` and `ripples.ts`: distinct feathered studio reflections against a dimmer environment, contact shadow, and bounded surface waves.
- `src/soft-body/audio.ts`: synthesized body tones, soft filtered noise, varied tap/release envelopes, and one reusable gesture texture. Hand speed controls its volume; stretch and twist change pitch and tone. At most three transient groups and one gesture voice play together. A short fade avoids hard cuts on mute/reset; ended nodes disconnect, and disposal closes the context. Enabling sound repeatedly never adds another output connection, and pending audio resumes cannot undo mute or disposal. Audio resources are created only after sound is enabled.

The solver uses a fixed 120 Hz timestep with at most six catch-up steps per frame. Rendering starts at a pixel ratio of at least 1.5 for smoother edges, caps it at 1.75, and lowers it under sustained slow frames. Once the entrance and ripples finish and all motion remains below small thresholds for 0.3 seconds, the toy stops scheduling frames. Pointer and keyboard input wake it immediately; resize, reset, and resuming the page also redraw it. Input uses raycasting, pointer capture, bounded drags, and cleanup on cancellation, lost capture, blur, and hidden pages. Hidden pages pause physics and audio. These are stylized soft bodies with anchored bases, not general-purpose material simulations.

## Validation

For ASCII Tide and Liquid Light, open `/qa/fields-smoke.html` on the local development server and select **Run checks**. This exercises actual Canvas/WebGL rendering, synthetic touch gestures, reduced motion, resize preservation, GPU-buffer stability, resource cleanup, and graphics-context loss/recovery. [Field validation results and limits](docs/FIELD_VALIDATION.md) records the robustness pass. Unit tests also cover rapid taps and complete strokes between frames, input floods, capture failures, and keyboard/pointer handoff.

The automated suite covers front/side/back inward dents, top compression and sideways expansion, off-center torque, keyboard torsion, spring rebound, slow cushion recovery, measurable upward/sideways surface travel, pressure unloading during pulls, gradual stiffness recovery after release, repeated extreme drags and unloaded re-grabs on moving skin, rapid taps, reset, volume/floor constraints, surface curvature continuity, combined compression/twist inversion, catching a rebound, smooth ripple onset and direction on deformed skin, reduced motion, entrance bounds and drag stability, sustained settling and wake behavior, and asynchronous toy lifecycle behavior. Collection checks also verify closed welded topology, the Loop's empty center and hittable rim, five Star arms, twelve Dumpling pleats, and actual-surface press/pull/recovery on every new profile. Multi-contact tests measure both sides of a pinch and stretch on the actual skin, simultaneous press/pull, partial release, five-contact extreme drags, rapid finger replacement, capture cancellation, duplicate IDs, contact limits, and global cleanup.

Development-only canvas `data-diagnostics` reports backend, active frame rate, sleeping state, render dimensions, current compression, twist, target dent depth, local deformation, volume, active pointer/contact counts, keyboard state, and renderer resource counts. While sleeping, the frame counter remains fixed and the frame-rate value describes the last active frames. These diagnostics are omitted from production. Reload after editing an already-mounted engine module to recreate its renderer.

Browser checks include WebGPU and forced WebGL2, toy switching, pointer/keyboard input, controls, and phone viewport layouts. Trusted Chromium touch emulation at 390×844 covers a non-primary second finger, simultaneous pinch/pull, lifting only one finger, cancellation, reset, blur, and keeping the page unscrolled and unzoomed. Physical phone hardware and device-level reduced-motion settings have not been tested; reduced motion is covered in the automated suite.

The script starts and closes an isolated headless browser and writes screenshots/results to ignored `qa/artifacts/`.
